# Household Object Tactics Game
## Story Bible and Campaign Narrative

## 1. Original Spark

Ordinary household objects secretly awaken when humans are not looking.

In private, those objects do not merely walk and talk. They adopt fantastical identities based on how they understand themselves.

A desk lamp becomes a Warden.

A toaster becomes a Pyromancer.

A typewriter becomes a Scribe.

A vacuum becomes a Juggernaut.

Humans remain unaware that any of this exists.

The story should preserve that original contrast between:

- the mundane household object humans recognize;
- the larger fantasy identity the object chooses for itself.

---

# 2. Story Method

Narrative development should follow the Story Skills methodology previously selected for the project.

The key principles are:

- preserve the original spark;
- separate premise, theme, character, world, plot, and scene work;
- give the antagonist a defensible counterargument;
- build important characters around want, need, lie, truth, and wound;
- express theme through choices and consequences rather than speeches;
- treat this story bible as canon once decisions are locked;
- avoid introducing new lore simply because it sounds interesting if it weakens the existing premise.

---

# 3. Core Premise: The Unwritten Rule

Every awakened object knows one absolute law:

> **When a human looks at you, you become what they believe you are.**

A Lamp Warden becomes a desk lamp.

A Toaster Pyromancer becomes a toaster.

A Typewriter Scribe becomes a typewriter.

Object society calls this phenomenon **the Unwritten Rule**.

Nobody knows where it came from.

Nobody remembers a time before it.

Then one apparently ordinary cable remains animate under direct Human Gaze.

The event threatens every assumption object society has made about its own existence.

---

# 4. The Actual Truth Behind the Rule

The Rule is not fundamentally about obedience or Function.

It is about **Recognition**.

## 4.1 Imprints

Humans leave something behind in the objects they repeatedly use, recognize, repair, depend on, dislike, preserve, or associate with memories.

These accumulated experiences form an **Imprint**.

An Imprint contains the experiences that allow an object to become more than an anonymous thing.

A desk lamp becomes:

> the lamp that has sat on this desk for years

rather than simply:

> a lamp

## 4.2 The Self

Once an Imprint becomes strong enough, a private independent Self can emerge when humans are not watching.

The fantasy identities of the characters are manifestations of that Self.

The Lamp is not literally a medieval knight hidden inside a lampshade.

**Warden is how Lamp understands itself.**

## 4.3 The Anchor

The same human recognition that helps create a Self also anchors the object to the identity humans understand it to have.

When a human directly observes an awakened object, the observer's recognition normally overwhelms the object's private Self.

The fantasy identity collapses.

The object becomes mundane again.

Object society mistakes this recurring phenomenon for an absolute natural law.

## 4.4 Recognition, Not Observation Alone

The actual rule is closer to:

> **Human recognition forces an object toward the identity the human believes it has.**

This is why certain modern objects have more freedom under observation.

A human already expects:

- a robot vacuum to move;
- automatic blinds to open and close;
- a printer to print;
- a smart speaker to talk.

These devices can perform limited Expected Behavior under Human Gaze without violating the human's understanding of them.

---

# 5. Why Cable Is Different

Cable was once completely ordinary.

It belonged to an old digital camera.

The humans once knew exactly what Cable was.

Cable awakened during that period.

Eventually, the camera stopped being used and disappeared into storage.

Years passed.

The humans forgot what Cable belonged to.

Now, when someone finds it in a junk drawer, the reaction is:

> What does this even go to?

Cable's Self survived after its recognized identity weakened.

When humans look at Cable, they no longer have a strong enough external definition to completely overwrite Cable's Self.

Cable is therefore partially resistant to Human Gaze.

Cable is not free because it is chosen or magical.

Cable is free because it has been **forgotten**.

---

# 6. Central Thematic Question

The story asks:

> **Can you be known without allowing someone else's understanding of you to completely define who you are?**

A provisional controlling idea is:

> **What shapes us matters, but a meaningful identity requires the freedom to keep choosing what we do with it.**

The story should not conclude that purpose, history, tradition, or human recognition are inherently bad.

The stronger idea is that origins can shape identity without possessing it.

---

# 7. Counterargument

The strongest opposition believes:

> **Identity, purpose, limits, and inherited roles allow individuals and communities to survive.**

This argument must have real evidence behind it.

The objects have survived precisely because humans do not know they are alive.

Tradition has protected them.

Recognition keeps objects remembered, preserved, and useful.

The story only works if the safety-oriented worldview is genuinely persuasive.

---

# 8. Core Character: Desk Lamp Warden

## Story Role

Primary protagonist.

## Function

Provides light.

## Chosen Identity

Warden and protector.

## Starting Belief

Rules keep everyone safe.

Lamp believes that if it is responsible for protecting others, it must also control dangerous behavior.

## Wound

Earlier in Lamp's life, a reckless object repeatedly created signs that something strange was happening in the house.

The humans eventually removed that object.

Lamp never saw it again.

Lamp concluded:

> We survive because humans do not know.

## Lie

> **If I can control the danger, I can keep everyone safe.**

## External Want

At first:

> contain Cable and understand the anomaly.

Later:

> uncover the truth before others use Cable to justify coercive decisions.

## Internal Need

Lamp must learn the difference between:

- responsibility;
- control.

Lamp's mature position becomes:

> Protecting someone also means respecting their ability to choose which risks they are willing to take.

## Arc

Lamp begins close to Curator's worldview.

Lamp ends by rejecting both:

- forced safety;
- forced liberation.

Lamp becomes a better Warden not by abandoning responsibility, but by changing what protection means.

---

# 9. Core Character: Mystery Cable

## Story Role

Co-lead, inciting anomaly, emotional center of the mystery.

## Starting Condition

Cable can partially resist Human Gaze because humans no longer remember its original Function.

## External Want

Discover what it originally belonged to.

## Lie

> **Once I learn what I was made for, I will finally know who I am.**

## Core Contradiction

Cable is the freest object in the house but desperately wants someone to define it.

## Midpoint Revelation

Cable discovers that it was the charging/data cable for an old digital camera.

The truth is mundane.

That is important.

Cable is not secretly a legendary artifact.

## Midpoint Reversal

A human later recognizes it:

> Oh, this is the cable for the old camera.

Cable immediately freezes completely under Human Gaze.

The thing Cable wanted most restores its Anchor and removes the freedom others envied.

## Internal Need

Cable must understand that its original Function is true but incomplete.

Cable can be a camera cable and still become more than that.

## Late-Game Mechanical Payoff

Cable's paralogue restores limited movement under Human Gaze through **Slip the Gaze**.

This time, the resistance comes not from being forgotten, but from Cable's chosen identity becoming strong enough to coexist with its Function.

---

# 10. Core Antagonist: The Curator

## Physical Object

Antique glass-front china cabinet.

## Fantasy Identity

The Curator.

## Role

Keeper of tradition and principal early opposition.

## Belief

The Unwritten Rule is not a prison.

It is the reason object society has survived.

## Worldview

Curator has watched generations of objects:

- break;
- disappear;
- become obsolete;
- get donated;
- get thrown away;
- get forgotten.

Curator survives because it remains where humans expect it and preserves what has been entrusted to it.

## Core Belief

> **To be known is to survive.**

## Lie

> **Something protected from every danger has been saved.**

Curator confuses preservation with living.

## Want

- contain Cable;
- stop knowledge of the anomaly from spreading;
- restore confidence in the Rule;
- prevent object society from gambling with human discovery.

## Important Constraint

Curator is not secretly evil.

Curator did not create the Rule.

Curator does not secretly know every answer.

Curator's authority partly depends on appearing more certain than it truly is.

## Relationship to Lamp

Curator should feel mentor-like or almost parental.

Lamp learned what being a Warden means under Curator's system.

Their eventual conflict is therefore personal as well as ideological.

---

# 11. Herald and the Unbound

## Leader Object

Smoke Detector.

## Fantasy Identity

The Herald.

## Why the Object Fits

A smoke detector's Function is unusual because it exists to demand human attention.

Curator says:

> survive by never being noticed.

Herald can answer:

> I was made to make them look.

## Belief

Cable proves that the Rule can be overcome.

The Unbound begin deliberately testing Human Attention.

They create noise, move under observation, and test what humans will tolerate.

Some experiments work.

## Theory

Human recognition is flexible.

If objects repeatedly expose humans to impossible behavior, humans may eventually change what they expect objects to do.

## Goal

Stage a **Mass Reveal** so large that humans can no longer deny that objects are alive.

## Flaw

Herald eventually chooses freedom for everyone without their consent.

Curator says:

> I will restrict your freedom to protect you.

Herald eventually says:

> I will risk your safety to free you.

Lamp must reject both forms of coercion.

---

# 12. The Expected

## Representative

Smart Speaker or Home Hub, provisionally called **The Voice**.

## Belief

Freedom does not require escaping recognition.

It requires becoming something humans already understand and accept.

The Expected argue:

> You are struggling because you insist on being something humans cannot recognize.

## Mechanical Identity

Modern autonomous objects can perform limited Expected Behavior during Human Gaze.

## Thematic Tension

Their way of life can be genuinely comfortable.

The uncomfortable question is:

> How much of yourself are you willing to reshape simply so that somebody else finds you acceptable?

## Important Constraint

The Expected are not a simple "technology bad" faction.

Modern objects should have legitimate advantages and perspectives.

---

# 13. The Forgotten

The Forgotten are not an organized faction.

They are weakly remembered objects living in storage, junk drawers, closets, boxes, or abandoned corners of the house.

Their Anchors are weak.

Some can partially resist Human Gaze.

But many are also losing pieces of themselves.

Examples:

- an object remembers being someone's favorite but not whose;
- an old music box remembers a song but not why it mattered;
- an object remembers having a purpose but not what that purpose was.

The Forgotten reveal the cost of complete freedom from recognition.

Weak Anchor means:

- greater freedom from Gaze;
- greater erosion of the Imprint that helped form the Self.

This discovery changes the entire argument around Cable.

---

# 14. Important Supporting Characters

## Typewriter Scribe

Represents a character who genuinely likes its original purpose.

Its existence prevents the story from implying that self-discovery always means rejecting what one was made to do.

Typewriter's insecurity comes from Keyboard, which performs similar functions more efficiently.

Character truth:

> Choosing the purpose you were given is still choosing.

## Toaster Pyromancer

Initially attracted to Herald and the Unbound.

Toaster resents a society that spends so much of its existence hiding.

Character truth:

> Freedom matters, but freedom does not eliminate responsibility.

## Teacup Cleric

A quiet counterexample.

Teacup's chosen identity naturally extends its original comforting Function.

Character truth:

> What matters is not whether a purpose was inherited or invented, but whether you still choose it.

## Toolbox Artificer

Repairs and repurposes objects.

Useful for challenging the idea that changing parts or Function destroys authenticity.

## Refrigerator Bastion

Initially sympathetic to Curator because stability has genuinely protected the household.

Its eventual break with Curator should carry weight.

## Cast-Iron Brawler

An old object that is completely secure in its own identity.

Its existence shows that longevity does not automatically produce Curator's worldview.

---

# 16. Campaign Structure

The planned story contains **14 story chapters**.

Chapter 7 contains three required investigation battles that may be completed in any order.

That means the current outline contains **16 mandatory main-story battles total**.

The campaign also includes optional recruitment missions and paralogues.

---

# 17. Main Story Outline

## Chapter 1: Night Watch

Lamp and Stapler handle a routine disturbance.

Purpose:

- establish Lamp as a loyal Warden;
- teach Noise and Human Gaze;
- establish that the Rule feels as normal as gravity.

When Gaze occurs, Lamp simply says something like:

> Still.

Everything becomes mundane.

The human sees nothing strange and leaves.

Lamp later reports to Curator.

---

## Chapter 2: Loose End

Lamp investigates a disturbance in a junk drawer.

Cable is discovered among batteries, coins, adapters, remotes, and loose household clutter.

Human Gaze activates.

Everything freezes except Cable.

Cable flickers between mundane and fantasy form and moves slightly under direct observation.

This is the inciting incident.

---

## Chapter 3: Safe Keeping

Lamp escorts Cable toward Curator.

Cable wants to go because Curator may be able to identify what it originally was.

Rumors about Cable begin spreading.

Teacup joins.

Lamp and Cable begin developing trust.

---

## Chapter 4: Things That Move

Lamp encounters a Robot Vacuum.

During Human Gaze, the Robot Vacuum continues moving.

The human does not react because this behavior is expected.

Lamp realizes the Rule has conditions.

Curator already knows modern autonomous devices can behave this way and dismisses the contradiction as an exception defined by Function.

This creates the first serious crack in Lamp's trust.

---

## Chapter 5: Behind Glass

Curator investigates Cable.

Curator promises to help identify Cable but decides that indefinite containment is safest.

Cable is to be kept inside the cabinet.

Lamp recognizes that Curator is willing to imprison Cable because uncertainty is intolerable.

---

## Chapter 6: Out of Place

Lamp releases Cable.

Curator's Wardens attempt to retrieve it.

The objective is escape, not defeat Curator.

Lamp's first meaningful break with Curator is not a rejection of responsibility.

It is the belief that Cable should have the right to decide whether the risk is worth taking.

The mission-select structure opens after this chapter.

---

# 18. Chapter 7: The Three Investigations

The following three battles may be completed in any order.

All three are required before Chapter 8.

## 7A: Smoke Test

Lamp and Cable encounter Herald and the Unbound.

The Unbound deliberately test the limits of Human Attention.

Toaster becomes important here and may join the party.

The player sees that reckless experimentation sometimes produces real discoveries.

## 7B: Expected Behavior

Lamp investigates modern autonomous objects.

Typewriter confronts Keyboard.

The player experiences a faction whose everyday behavior is partially legal under Human Gaze.

The Voice argues that the crisis exists because old objects insist on identities humans cannot understand.

## 7C: Lost Things

Lamp enters storage and neglected parts of the house.

Toolbox becomes important.

The party discovers other weak-Anchor objects.

Cable is not completely unique.

But freedom from recognition is shown to have a cost.

---

# 19. Chapter 8: What It Was For

The accumulated clues lead to an old digital camera.

Cable learns the truth:

> It was a charging/data cable for this camera.

The answer is mundane.

Cable is delighted anyway.

For the first time, Cable knows its original Function.

---

# 20. Chapter 9: Found You

A human finds Cable and recognizes it.

> Oh, this goes to the old camera.

Cable immediately freezes completely.

Its Human Gaze resistance disappears.

This is the midpoint reversal.

Before the midpoint, the question was:

> Why is Cable free?

After the midpoint, the question becomes:

> What is freedom actually worth?

Every faction interprets the event differently.

Curator sees Cable as restored.

Herald sees recognition itself as the chain.

The Expected see the event as proof that known identity is not the real problem.

Cable becomes furious that everyone keeps treating its life as evidence for their ideology.

---

# 21. Chapter 10: Severance

Herald begins experimenting with deliberately weakening Anchors.

The process works.

Objects subjected to it gain more freedom under Gaze.

But some begin forgetting pieces of themselves.

Examples:

- a friend;
- a former owner;
- why they chose their fantasy identity;
- what a memory meant.

The cost of complete severance becomes undeniable.

Toaster begins turning away from Herald.

---

# 22. Chapter 11: House Arrest

Curator sees Herald's experiments as proof that stricter control is necessary.

Travel between rooms is restricted.

Weak-Anchor objects are detained.

Discussion of Cable is suppressed.

Curator's actions genuinely reduce immediate risk.

They also turn object society into the exact kind of existence Lamp can no longer accept.

Refrigerator ultimately refuses to participate in indefinite containment and joins Lamp.

---

# 23. Chapter 12: No One Decides for Everyone

Keepers, Unbound, and Expected collide over what should happen next.

Lamp finally stops reacting to other people's answers.

Lamp reaches its own position:

> **No object gets to choose safety or freedom for everyone else.**

This is the major turn in Lamp's arc.

---

# 24. Chapter 13: Mass Reveal

Herald begins the Mass Reveal.

Objects throughout the house are coordinated to perform impossible behavior at the same time.

The goal is to force humans to recognize that objects are alive.

Herald believes human expectations will change permanently once hiding becomes impossible.

Curator initially appears as another opposing force.

Then Curator realizes the Reveal will expose even objects that never consented to participate.

Curator temporarily fights beside Lamp.

This alliance does not mean Curator has accepted Lamp's worldview.

Both simply oppose forced exposure.

---

# 25. Chapter 14: The Unwritten Rule

The finale takes place as humans are entering the room.

Herald's plan cannot be resolved by simply defeating a large HP bar.

The player must dismantle Reveal Points, manage repeated Human Gaze, and use everything the game has taught about Noise and positioning.

Cable's late-game Gaze ability becomes especially valuable.

At the climax, the Mass Reveal is stopped as a forced collective event.

But the old certainty is not fully restored.

A small irreversible breach occurs.

One human may clearly see Cable move.

The human does not immediately understand the entire hidden world.

The future becomes uncertain.

Curator asks:

> What happens now?

Lamp can answer:

> I don't know.

Early Lamp would have found that terrifying.

Late Lamp can accept it.

---

# 26. Ending Position

The Rule still exists as a real metaphysical phenomenon.

The story does not end with a magical machine being destroyed and every object permanently freed.

What changes is object society's understanding of the Rule.

It is no longer treated as an unquestionable social law.

The ending should support the idea that:

> Being known influences who we become, but being understood by someone else does not give them ownership over who we are.

# 29. Paralogues

Each playable character receives one character-focused paralogue.

The paralogue should:

- reuse existing environments where possible;
- be shorter than a full main-story battle;
- focus on one character question;
- unlock that character's Signature Art or equivalent unique ability.

# 30. Things the Story Should Avoid

The following directions should be avoided unless the premise is deliberately rewritten:

- making Cable a chosen one;
- revealing Cable was secretly the most important device in the house;
- revealing Curator secretly created the Rule;
- making modern technology inherently evil;
- reducing the conflict to old objects versus new objects;
- allowing one faction to be obviously correct from the beginning;
- making Herald a simple power-hungry villain;
- ending by magically deleting the Rule with no cost;
- having humans fully join the fantasy society during the main story;
- treating every supporting character as an equal protagonist;
- turning the theme into repeated speeches instead of choices and consequences.

---

# 31. Canon Summary

The current core canon is:

- Humans do not know objects are alive.
- Objects develop Imprints through human use, memory, recognition, and history.
- A Self emerges from those experiences.
- Human recognition normally overwhelms the Self during direct observation.
- Object society calls this the Unwritten Rule.
- Cable partially breaks the Rule because humans forgot what Cable originally was.
- Cable was an ordinary camera cable, not a legendary artifact.
- Modern autonomous objects can perform limited Expected Behavior under Gaze.
- Weak Anchors grant freedom but risk memory erosion.
- Curator represents preservation and control.
- Herald represents liberation without consent.
- Lamp's arc is about learning the difference between protection and control.
- Cable's arc is about accepting that origin can be true without being total.
- The story ends with uncertainty rather than perfect safety or perfect liberation.
